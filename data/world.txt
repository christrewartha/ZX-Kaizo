; The levels, in the order they are played. Each level is one screen: start
; on the left at S, reach the exit E on the right. Dying sends you back to S.
;
;   level <name>                     starts a level; the name is shown
;                                    under the play area
;   enemy <walker|flyer> <x> <y> <min> <max> <speed px/frame> <colour>
;                                    up to 4 per level; walkers patrol x
;                                    between min and max, flyers y
; Colours: blue red magenta green cyan yellow white
;
; The enemy lines are followed by exactly 22 rows of 32 cells (trailing
; spaces may be trimmed):
;   ' ' empty   '#' brick   'X' block   '=' one-way platform
;   '^' floor spikes   'v' ceiling spikes   'E' exit   'S' player start
; S marks the bottom-left cell of the player's 16x16 sprite.
;
; The screen edges are walls, the top is a ceiling, and falling out of
; the bottom is fatal. A walker standing on row r has y = r * 8 - 16.
;
; Jump distances to design with (tools/check_levels.ps1 has the exact
; physics and proves each level can be finished):
; - A full jump rises 34 px, so a ledge 4 rows up is the limit and 3 rows
;   is comfortable.
; - A running jump covers 54 px: gaps of 4 columns are easy, 6 are hard
;   and 7 need a perfect run-up and take-off.
; - A tapped jump rises 13 px and covers 22 px.
; - Wall jumps climb a shaft 4 columns wide. Take off with both walls
;   beside you: a wall jump into open air leaves nothing to kick off next.
